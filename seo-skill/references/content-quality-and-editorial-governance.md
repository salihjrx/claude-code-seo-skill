# Content Quality and Editorial Governance

## Before drafting
Require an audience, task, intent, differentiated contribution, evidence plan, reviewer, conversion path, locale and update trigger. If no meaningful contribution can be identified, do not publish a page merely to target a keyword.

## Content evidence ledger
For every material factual claim, record source, publication/update date, confidence, scope and reviewer. Distinguish firsthand observation, official documentation, expert interpretation and opinion. Never fabricate case studies, tests, quotations, author credentials or personal experience.

## Useful content test
Ask:
- Does this solve the user's task fully and efficiently?
- What original evidence, data, expertise or practical utility does it add?
- Are important limitations and alternatives disclosed?
- Are claims current and sourced?
- Is the page meaningfully distinct from existing pages?
- Is the next action clear and appropriate?
- Does the author/reviewer have relevant experience, and is it represented honestly?

## Programmatic content
Only scale a page template when each generated page has reliable unique data and distinct user value. Set minimum data thresholds, deduplicate near-identical pages, review samples across segments, monitor indexation and outcomes, and automatically suppress stale or low-value pages. Never use location/name swaps as a substitute for useful local information.

## Refresh and retire
Refresh when facts, inventory, law, software, pricing or user needs change. Merge only when intent overlaps and the destination genuinely serves the old page's users. Redirect retired content to a close equivalent; otherwise consider an honest 404/410. Preserve URL history and measure post-change effects.
